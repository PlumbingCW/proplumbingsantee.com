Planning a water heater installation

A new installation starts with the household: how many people, how many bathrooms and when they use hot water. A heater too small leaves someone cold, and one too large wastes fuel. The plumber also checks fuel supply, venting, space and the condition of the water lines before ordering anything.

If your current heater has failed already, water heater replacement covers the fast swap, and this page covers planned work.

What a proper installation includes

These details separate a good install from a rushed one.

  • ✓
    Expansion controlA closed system needs a place for thermal expansion to go. An expansion tank handles it.
  • ✓
    Drain pan and drain lineA pan catches slow leaks and carries them to a safe spot.
  • ✓
    Seismic strappingTanks in earthquake country should be strapped to the wall. Padre Dam's hazard plan names the San Andreas and Elsinore faults as risks to the region.
  • ✓
    Proper ventingGas units need draft and clearances checked.
  • ✓
    Shutoff valve and unionsNew shutoffs and unions make future service easier.

Hard water and permits in Santee

Padre Dam Municipal Water District's 2023 water quality report lists hardness of roughly 9 to 18 grains per gallon depending on the plant. At that level, a drain valve for yearly flushing and an accessible anode rod are worth planning for at install time.

The City of Santee has a separate page for solar water heating permits, and it does not list conventional water heaters on its page about work not requiring a permit. Ask the Planning and Building department what your project needs, because permit rules vary.

Sources: Padre Dam Municipal Water District, 2023 Water Quality Report. City of Santee Planning and Building.

Choosing the right location

The location of a water heater affects its performance and its life. A garage is common, but a unit in a garage needs protection from vehicles and a raised platform where code requires it for gas models. An interior closet needs combustion air for gas units and room to service it.

Keep the unit near the fixtures that use the most hot water. Long pipe runs mean long waits and lost heat, and insulation on the first few feet of hot line helps. If you are moving the heater, tell the plumber early so the gas, water and vent routes can be planned together.

Code requirements for a new water heater

A new water heater is installed under the California Plumbing Code, based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). That covers the relief valve and discharge pipe, venting for gas units and a drain pan where a leak could damage finished space.

Ask the plumber about thermal expansion control and the permit. The City of Santee page on work not requiring a permit does not mention water heaters, so check with Planning and Building before the work begins.
