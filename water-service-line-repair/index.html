When a service line leak needs a fast response

A leaking service line wastes treated water, softens soil and can undermine paving. If the yard is soggy, pressure has dropped, or the meter spins with the house shut off, call the 24/7 line. We schedule same-day visits when available.

Close the house shutoff valve to see whether the meter still turns. If it does, the leak is outside, and the plumber will confirm where.

Signs the service line is leaking

Service line failures show up outside before inside.

  • ✓
    Wet or sunken groundA patch that stays wet in dry weather, often between the meter and the house.
  • ✓
    Pressure dropWeak flow at every fixture suggests a loss before the house.
  • ✓
    Meter moves with the main offClosing the house valve and seeing movement points to the buried line.
  • ✓
    Rising billsUnexplained increases in usage on a steady household.
  • ✓
    Discolored or dirty waterPipe damage can let sediment in during pressure changes.

Who owns what between the meter and the house

Padre Dam Municipal Water District maintains about 393 miles of water mains across its service area, including Santee. In general, a utility maintains the main and the meter, and the owner is responsible for the line from the meter to the house. Ask Padre Dam where responsibility changes on your property before any digging.

Call 811 before any excavation near buried lines. A plumber will arrange this as part of a service line job.

Source: Padre Dam Municipal Water District hazard mitigation annex (San Diego County, 2023).

Repair or replace the service line

The choice turns on the pipe material, the length and the damage.

OptionMethodBest when
Spot repairDig down and replace a short sectionA single leak, sound pipe elsewhere
Replacement in a trenchLay new pipe along the old pathOld, corroded or multiple leaks
Trenchless replacementPull new pipe through the old pathDriveways, landscaping or hardscape to preserve

How a service line job runs

Careful sequencing reduces yard damage.

  1. 1

    Locate the leak

    Pressure testing and acoustic tools find the problem area.

  2. 2

    Confirm utility boundaries

    The plumber checks the meter and shutoff and the line path.

  3. 3

    Excavate or pull

    The damaged section is exposed, or new pipe is pulled through.

  4. 4

    Test and backfill

    The new section is pressure tested before the trench is closed.

Who owns the pipe, and which rules apply

The service line between the meter and your house is generally the owner's responsibility, while the meter and the main belong to the water district. Repairs follow the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), and trench work may need a permit.

Call before you dig. California's 811 system, run through Underground Service Alert, marks buried utilities for free, and a plumber will not trench before they are marked.
