El Cajon homes are old enough to ask for a plan

El Cajon incorporated on November 12, 1912, with 123 of 158 electors voting yes. Its population reached 1,471 by 1940, climbed to about 5,600 in the postwar rush, and hit 37,618 by 1960 when the city had grown to 9.8 square miles. Today it covers 14.4 square miles and about 102,337 residents.

That 1945 to 1960 surge built a large share of the housing. A house from that period is now past seventy, and its drain line may be cast iron or clay. When a line backs up twice, a camera look before any quote tells you whether you need cleaning or repair.

Before we arrive: active leak or burst pipe

If water is running where it should not, these steps limit the damage while the plumber heads over.

Close the main

Turn the main valve clockwise at the house or at the meter box.

Drain the lines

Open a low faucet to relieve pressure and empty the pipes.

Kill power near water

Only if the panel is dry and you can reach it safely.

Move valuables and call

Lift items off wet floors and call our 24/7 line. See burst pipe repair.

Codes and utilities that apply in El Cajon

Plumbing in El Cajon follows the California Plumbing Code, based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). Parts of the city are served by Padre Dam Municipal Water District and others by Helix Water District, so the district named on your bill tells you whom to contact about the meter or the main.

The City of El Cajon sets its own permit process. Ask its building staff what a larger job needs, and tell the plumber which district serves your street.

Water service and your bill

The City of El Cajon says Helix Water District delivers water to El Cajon, La Mesa and Lemon Grove. Padre Dam Municipal Water District's service area also includes El Cajon, so which district bills you depends on your address.

Check your bill to see which district you use, then look up that district's annual water quality report for hardness. We do not quote a figure for Helix here because we have not confirmed one from its own report.
