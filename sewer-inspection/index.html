What a sewer camera inspection shows

A sewer inspection feeds a waterproof camera on a flexible cable through your cleanout and down the line toward the street connection. The monitor shows the pipe interior live, and the cable counter marks how far in each problem sits. You see cracks, offset joints, root hairs, scale and sagging sections that hold water.

The footage replaces guessing. Instead of cleaning a line again and hoping, you know whether the pipe needs sewer line cleaning, a patch or something bigger, and you can show the recording to anyone who needs to see it.

Good reasons to scope a line

A camera visit costs less than most repairs and often prevents a wrong one.

Buying an older home

A pre-purchase scope shows the condition of a line you cannot see. Much of Santee grew from under 2,000 residents in 1950 to 25,750 in 1970, so many lines are decades old.

Repeat blockages

Two backups in a year suggest a defect, not bad luck.

Before landscaping or a remodel

Roots, trees and added bathrooms put new demand on a line. Know its condition first.

After a repair

A post-work scope proves the new section is joined and flowing as expected.

What the camera can tell you

Each finding leads to a different action.

The footage shows where each defect sits by distance from the cleanout.

How the inspection goes

It is quick and keeps disruption small.

  1. 1

    Access the line

    The plumber uses an outdoor cleanout, or removes a toilet if there is none.

  2. 2

    Run the camera

    The head moves downstream while the monitor records.

  3. 3

    Mark the problems

    Distances are noted so a repair can target the right spot.

  4. 4

    Review the footage

    You see it together, and the plumber explains what each finding means in plain words.

What local ownership boundaries mean for a scope

The camera covers your private lateral, which runs from the house to the connection. Padre Dam Municipal Water District maintains about 169 miles of wastewater mains beyond that point, and it handles anything found past the connection. If the footage shows a problem in the district main, the plumber will say so and point you to the district.

For a home purchase, ask the seller for the cleanout location, and schedule the scope before the inspection contingency ends. A clean recording gives you a baseline, and a bad one gives you something real to negotiate with.

Source: Padre Dam Municipal Water District hazard mitigation annex (San Diego County, 2023).
