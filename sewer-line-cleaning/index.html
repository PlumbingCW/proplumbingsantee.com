Sewer backup: what to do first

Sewage coming up through a floor drain or tub is a health issue as well as a plumbing one, and it gets worse with every flush. Call the 24/7 line now and we schedule a same-day visit when a technician is free. Meanwhile, stop all water use in the house, including the washer, and keep people and pets out of the affected room.

Wear rubber boots and gloves if you must walk through it, and do not run fans or touch outlets near standing water.

Stop every drain

No flushing, no showers, no laundry. All of it ends up in the same blocked line.

Keep away from wet outlets

If water reaches a plug or a power strip, leave it alone and switch off that circuit at the panel only if the panel is dry.

Open the cleanout cap carefully

Only if you can do it safely. A backed-up line can release pressure and spray, so stand to the side and tell the plumber you did it.

Take photos for your records

Pictures of the water level and affected items help if you later talk to your insurer or landlord.

What sewer line cleaning covers

The sewer line is the pipe that carries everything from your house to the connection with the district main. Cleaning it means running a heavy-duty cable or a jetting hose through the whole run, usually from a cleanout outside the home. A smaller sink cable cannot reach that far or cut through roots and packed paper.

It is a different job from ordinary drain cleaning. The cable is larger, the access is outdoors, and the result is verified with water flow from every fixture. When the cleaning finds a break or a bellied pipe, the next step is a camera, which is what sewer inspection covers.

Why Santee sewer lines back up

The causes are mostly local and mostly predictable. These are the four we see first.

Roots at the joints

Roots seek water and enter the line where joints open. A root mass catches paper and the line backs up slowly, then all at once.

Older pipe interiors

Cast iron and clay develop rough walls over decades. Grease and wipes snag on them. Santee's main growth came between the 1950s and 1970s, so many lines are at that age.

Flushed wipes

Wipes labeled flushable do not break down like paper. They collect at bends and form a plug.

Wet-weather loading

Heavy rain adds groundwater and runoff to the system. Padre Dam's own plan records the 2010 Sycamore Creek flooding that inundated its influent pump station.

How we clear a main sewer line

The work follows a fixed order so the result can be proven.

  1. 1

    Find the cleanout

    The plumber locates the outdoor cleanout, or pulls a toilet when there is none.

  2. 2

    Run the cable

    A machine cable with a cutting head breaks through the blockage and scrapes the pipe walls.

  3. 3

    Flush and test

    Water is run through the line to confirm a full, fast flow.

  4. 4

    Recommend a camera if needed

    Repeat blockages or odd findings lead to a camera check, and hydro jetting when grease or scale is the cause.

Why cleanouts and traps matter for sewer work

The California Plumbing Code, based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), expects cleanouts at the base of stacks and at the property line on many installations. A cleanout gives the plumber a straight path for the cable or the jetter, and it avoids pulling a toilet.

If a cleanout is buried, cracked or missing, ask about adding or raising one. A proper cleanout turns the next sewer service into a short visit.
