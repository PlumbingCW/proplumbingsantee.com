What we verified about the Town Center district

Santee's Town Center is a planned district of about 700 acres beside the MTS trolley line. The city's Town Center Specific Plan guides land use there, and the 2025 update is available from the City of Santee.

The City's housing developments page lists Parc One "right next to Santee Trolley Square and the Trolley Station," and Riverwalk, a 218-unit townhome development, north of Town Center Community Park and the YMCA.

We have not confirmed drain sizes, grease interceptor locations or building ages for any Town Center property.

Sources: City of Santee Town Center Specific Plan page and housing developments page.

Three kinds of property, three kinds of plumbing call

A district with shops, restaurants and homes side by side produces different calls on the same street.

Restaurants and food service

Kitchen drains carry grease, and a line that clogs once usually clogs again. Regular cleaning and a hydro jetting visit clear buildup that a cable misses.

Retail and offices

Restrooms are the main load. Several tenants can share one drain, so a backup in one suite can show up in another. See commercial plumbing.

Townhomes and apartments

Stacked units share vertical drains. A slow drain on a lower floor may start above, so check with the property manager or association before booking.

Shared lines and who owns them

In multi-tenant buildings the lease or the association documents decide who pays for a shared drain. Ask the property manager before a plumber is called, and ask for the cleanout location so the plumber can reach the line.

If wastewater is backing up into the lowest fixtures in a building, stop using water in every unit and call right away. A blocked main affects every tenant connected to it.

What a commercial drain call looks like

The sequence is similar for most shared-drain calls.

  1. 1

    Describe the symptoms

    Tell the dispatcher which fixtures are affected and whether other tenants see the same thing.

  2. 2

    Locate the cleanout

    Find the access point so the plumber does not need to open walls.

  3. 3

    Clear and inspect

    Cable or jet the line, then run a camera to see the pipe's condition.

  4. 4

    Plan the follow-up

    Ask whether regular cleaning makes sense, especially for grease lines.

Before a tenant move-in or remodel

Ask the landlord for as-built drawings if they exist, count the fixtures the space will add, and find the shutoffs and cleanouts that serve it. A plumber can then size the work against the lines already in place, instead of finding limits mid-job.

Ask the City of Santee whether the work needs a building permit. The city publishes a list of work that does not, and anything off that list goes through the Planning and Building department.
