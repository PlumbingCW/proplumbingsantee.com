How a tankless heater works

A tankless heater fires a gas burner or an electric element only when water flows, then heats the water as it passes through a heat exchanger. There is no storage tank to keep hot, so the unit never runs out as long as demand stays within its rated flow. That is the main advantage over a tank.

The trade-offs are higher upfront cost, venting or electrical upgrades and regular descaling. See water heater installation for the full comparison with tank units.

Is tankless right for your home

The decision turns on four things.

Peak demand

Add up the flow of fixtures used at the same time. The unit must meet that flow at your incoming water temperature.

Fuel and venting

Gas models need proper venting and gas line size. Electric models need heavy circuits.

Space

Wall mounting frees floor area, but clearances still apply.

Water quality

Hard water scales the heat exchanger, so a flushing plan matters.

Hard water in Santee and what it does to a tankless heater

Padre Dam Municipal Water District's 2023 water quality report lists hardness of roughly 9 to 18 grains per gallon depending on the plant, which is firmly in the range where scale forms inside a heat exchanger. Scale narrows passages and lowers efficiency, and a unit can throw error codes.

The usual defense is an annual flush with a descaling solution, and some homes add a softener, see water softener and filtration. Check your manual for the flush schedule.

Source: Padre Dam Municipal Water District, 2023 Water Quality Report.

What a tankless flush involves

A flush is a short, straightforward visit.

  1. 1

    Isolate the unit

    The plumber closes the valves and connects a small pump and hoses.

  2. 2

    Circulate descaler

    The solution runs through the heat exchanger for the time the maker recommends.

  3. 3

    Rinse and restore

    The unit is flushed clean, filters are cleared and valves reopened.

  4. 4

    Test

    Flow and temperature are checked and fault codes cleared.

Tankless repairs we see most

Error codes are the usual signal. A code can point to a scaled heat exchanger, a failed flow sensor, an exhaust or vent problem, or an ignition fault. The plumber reads the code, checks the symptoms and tests the part before replacing anything.

Cold bursts in the middle of a shower often come from low flow rather than a failure. Aerators and showerheads clogged with scale can drop the flow below the unit's activation point. Cleaning them is a cheap first step before a service call.

If the unit is old and parts are scarce, we say so. A repair that costs most of a replacement is not good value, and we will show you both prices.

Manufacturer guidelines and code for tankless units

A tankless heater is installed to the maker's instructions and the California Plumbing Code, based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). Gas units need the right gas line size and approved venting. Condensing units need a condensate drain.

Following the maker's guidelines also protects the warranty. That includes the descaling schedule and the use of isolation valves for flushing.
