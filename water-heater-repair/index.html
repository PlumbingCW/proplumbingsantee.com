No hot water or a leaking tank right now

A heater that stops mid-shower is annoying. A tank that leaks is urgent. Call the 24/7 line either way, and we book a same-day visit when a technician is free. If water is pooling under the tank, shut the cold supply valve above it and, for a gas unit, turn the gas control to pilot or off.

Do not touch a tank that smells of gas. Leave the area and call your gas utility or 911 first, then call us.

What the symptom usually means

Most water heater trouble matches a short list of causes. A technician confirms the cause before repairing anything.

No hot water at all

Gas units often have a failed thermocouple or igniter. Electric units may have a tripped breaker or a dead element.

Lukewarm water

A failed lower element, a worn dip tube or heavy sediment can all do it.

Rumbling or popping

Sediment baked on the bottom of the tank boils and bangs. Flushing sometimes helps.

Water around the base

A leaking fitting can be repaired. A leaking tank body cannot.

Hard water and your tank

Padre Dam Municipal Water District's 2023 water quality report lists hardness of roughly 9 to 18 grains per gallon depending on the plant. Hard water drops minerals as it heats, which coats elements and builds sediment. The anode rod that protects the tank also wears faster in some water, and it is cheap to check.

If your heater is past ten years old and has never been flushed, a full tank of sediment may be the reason it struggles. A flush or an anode swap can add useful life if the tank is not already leaking.

Safety devices a repair must respect

A tank water heater is required to have a temperature and pressure relief valve with a discharge pipe, under the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). A repair should never cap, plug or bypass that valve.

Gas models also rely on the maker's instructions for venting and combustion air. A plumber follows those guidelines when testing the unit, and a failed test is a reason to stop and fix the cause.
