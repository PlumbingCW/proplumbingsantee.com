Overflowing toilet: what to do first

An overflowing toilet can soak a bathroom floor in minutes. Reach behind the toilet and turn the small supply valve clockwise to stop the refill, then lift the tank lid and hold the flapper down if water is still rising. Then call the 24/7 line, and we book same-day visits when a technician is free.

If the water is dark or smells of sewage, other drains may be backing up too, and the cause may be the main line.

Close the supply valve

The oval handle on the wall behind the toilet. Turn it clockwise until it stops.

Stop flushing

Each flush adds water to a bowl that has nowhere to go.

Protect the floor

Towels at the base buy time. Move rugs and mats out of the way.

Check other drains

If the tub gurgles or the sink backs up, call and mention it. See sewer line cleaning.

Common toilet problems and what they mean

Most toilet calls fall into a few categories.

  • ✓
    Constant runningA worn flapper or a fill valve that will not shut off wastes water all day.
  • ✓
    Weak flushMineral deposits under the rim holes or a low water level in the tank reduce flush power.
  • ✓
    Rocking toilet or leak at the baseA failed wax ring or a broken flange lets water escape and can rot the subfloor.
  • ✓
    Clog that a plunger cannot clearA deeper blockage needs a closet auger or a drain cable.
  • ✓
    Tank sweating or leakingA bad tank-to-bowl gasket or supply connection.

Hard water and toilet parts

Padre Dam Municipal Water District's 2023 water quality report lists hardness of roughly 9 to 18 grains per gallon, depending on the plant. Mineral buildup clogs the rim jets of toilets and stiffens the rubber parts inside the tank, so flappers and fill valves in the area tend to age quickly.

A vinegar soak helps with minor rim deposits, but heavy buildup needs a descaler or a part swap. A plumber can tell which in a minute.

Source: Padre Dam Municipal Water District, 2023 Water Quality Report.

Flow limits and standards on toilets

New toilets sold in California are limited to 1.28 gallons per flush, and many are certified under the EPA WaterSense program. The California Plumbing Code, based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), also sets rules for the flange, the supply connection and the venting behind a toilet.

If a repair keeps failing on an older high-volume toilet, a WaterSense model may save water and end the repeat visits.
