An emergency plumber in Santee, open 24/7

Most calls fall into a few groups: clogged drains, water heater failures, hidden leaks and burst pipes. If yours is something else, call anyway. The dispatcher will say what we can do and when.

What to do while you wait for the plumber

The first few minutes decide how much damage a plumbing emergency causes. These four steps cover most situations.

Find the main shutoff

Turn the main water valve clockwise to stop a leak or burst. It is often near the front hose bib or at the meter.

Stop using drains

If a drain is backing up, hold the laundry and do not flush. Water you add has nowhere to go.

Keep power away from water

Leave outlets and appliances alone in a wet area unless the panel is dry and you can switch off that circuit.

Smell gas? Leave first

For a gas smell near a water heater, get everyone out and call 911 or your gas utility before you call us.

Why Santee plumbing calls look the way they do

Santee grew fast after the Carlton Company bought 4,300 acres of ranch land in 1958. The population rose from under 2,000 in 1950 to 25,750 in 1970, and the city incorporated in 1980. Many homes are now fifty years old or more, so drain lines and supply pipes are aging and a camera look is often worth the money.

Water adds its own wear. Padre Dam Municipal Water District imports its supply, and its 2023 report lists hardness of roughly 9 to 18 grains per gallon depending on the treatment plant. Scale builds up in water heaters and faucets, which is why those two top the list of repairs.

Permits and what Santee says

The City of Santee's Planning and Building page says that stopping leaks, clearing blockages and fixing leaks in valves or fixtures is exempt from a building permit when no valves, pipes or fixtures are replaced or rearranged. Replacing a failed concealed pipe needs a permit, and emergency equipment replacements must be filed within two working business days.

Water heaters are not named on that page, so ask the Planning and Building department about your own project. Permit rules vary, and the plumber can tell you what to expect.

How to check any plumber's license

California's Contractors State License Board lets you look up a contractor by license number or business name and check the license status. Use it for any plumber you hire, including us. Ask for the license number before work begins and look it up yourself.

We are licensed and insured. We do not publish a license number on this site, so ask when you call and verify it with the board.
