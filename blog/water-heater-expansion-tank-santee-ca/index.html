Replacing a Water Heater Expansion Tank in Santee, CA

A failed expansion tank can push pressure past what your pipes and heater are built for. Here is how to test yours and when to call.

By the Santee Pro Plumbing team · Published November 12, 2026 · Updated November 12, 2026

The short answer

Replace a water heater expansion tank when it is waterlogged, when its air side no longer holds pressure, or when it is corroded or leaking. A quick test tells you in two minutes: tap the tank, then press the air valve on the side. A tank that sounds solid all over, or that spits water from the air valve, has failed.

A good tank lasts for years, but it does not last forever. If your pressure relief valve drips, or faucets spit and pipes bang when the heater fires, test the tank before replacing anything else. A bad tank is a cheap part with an expensive side effect.

What an expansion tank does

Water expands when it heats. In an open system, that extra volume pushes back into the city line. In a closed system, with a check valve or pressure reducing valve on the incoming line, it has nowhere to go. Pressure climbs inside your pipes, fixtures and water heater until something gives way or the relief valve opens.

The expansion tank is a small vessel with a rubber diaphragm and an air cushion. Heated water pushes into the tank against the air, and the pressure stays steady. Without that cushion, the relief valve is doing the tank's job, and it was not designed for daily duty.

Signs the tank has failed

Watch for these before the relief valve starts to drip.

  • ✓
    Relief valve dripping or spittingRepeated releases mean pressure is spiking after each heating cycle.
  • ✓
    Banging pipes at heat-upPressure swings make pipes knock and fittings creak.
  • ✓
    Pressure gauge jumpsA gauge on a hose bib that rises well above its resting number during a heat cycle points to a dead cushion.
  • ✓
    Tank feels full of waterA waterlogged tank is heavy and sounds dull when tapped from the top to the bottom.
  • ✓
    Water from the air valvePress the valve stem. Air should come out, never water.

How to test and replace the tank

Testing is safe for a homeowner. Replacement needs plumbing skill and, in many homes, a shutoff and drain-down.

  1. 1

    Test first

    Tap the tank from top to bottom and press the air valve. A healthy tank sounds hollow on top and solid at the bottom, and the valve gives only air.

  2. 2

    Shut off power or gas

    Turn off the breaker or move the gas control to pilot or off. Close the cold water supply to the heater.

  3. 3

    Relieve pressure and drain

    Open a hot faucet and drain a few gallons from the tank so the line is not under pressure.

  4. 4

    Swap and set the air charge

    Replace the tank on the cold line, then set its air pressure to match your home's static water pressure before it goes into service.

  5. 5

    Check for leaks and restore

    Turn the water on, open a hot faucet to bleed air, restore power or gas and watch the connections for drips.

A plumber can test pressure and replace the tank in one visit.

Setting the air charge correctly

The air side of the tank has to match your home's static water pressure with the tank disconnected from the water. If it is lower, the tank fills with water before the heater fires. If it is higher, the tank never takes the expansion. Either way it does not do its job.

Measure static pressure with a gauge on a hose bib, with no water running. Plumbing codes generally call for a pressure reducing valve when street pressure is high, with 80 psi as the usual ceiling. If your gauge reads near or above that, fix the pressure as well as the tank.

What the code says about thermal expansion

The California Plumbing Code, based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), calls for thermal expansion control where a water heater is on a closed system. A closed system has a check valve or a pressure regulator that stops expanding water from flowing back into the street.

Tank makers also include expansion guidelines in their manuals. Check yours, and ask a plumber to set the tank's air pressure to match your house pressure.

Santee note: pressure, permits and hard water

Santee has hillside neighborhoods such as Sky Ranch on Rattlesnake Mountain, the city's highest point at 1,198 feet. Pressure differs with elevation, so a house low on a slope can sit at a higher static pressure than one up the hill. A gauge on a hose bib is the way to know yours.

Padre Dam Municipal Water District's 2023 report reports hardness from roughly 9 to 18 grains per gallon, depending on the treatment plant. Scale can foul the relief valve and the drain valve, so test them when you replace the tank. The City of Santee does not list water heaters on its page about work not requiring a permit, so ask Planning and Building about your project.

Sources: City of Santee history page, City of Santee permit page, Padre Dam Municipal Water District, 2023 Water Quality Report.

When to call a plumber instead

Some jobs are worth handing off.

Gas water heaters

Gas connections and venting need care. A leak or a poor connection is dangerous.

Corroded fittings

Old fittings can snap when you loosen them, and a small job becomes a leak.

Pressure above 80 psi

The fix is a pressure reducing valve, which is a plumbing job.

Relief valve still leaks

A weeping valve may need replacement. Do not cap it.
