Why hillside homes ask about water pressure

Water pressure rises as elevation drops. Each 2.31 feet of drop adds about 1 psi, so a house at the bottom of a slope sees higher static pressure than one near the top, with the same utility main feeding both.

California plumbing codes call for a pressure reducing valve when static pressure passes 80 psi. A cheap hose-bib gauge tells you where your house sits. Readings above 80 psi are worth a plumber's attention, and very low readings point to a different cause, such as a partly closed valve.

A pressure and shutoff check you can do yourself

Fifteen minutes and a gauge from a hardware store are enough.

  • ✓
    Attach the gauge to a hose bibTurn off every fixture first so the reading is static pressure.
  • ✓
    Read it at a quiet hourPressure can rise overnight when neighbors use less water.
  • ✓
    Look for a pressure reducing valveIt is a bell-shaped brass fitting near the main shutoff. Many townhomes and houses have one.
  • ✓
    Test the main shutoffClose it fully and open a faucet. If the water keeps running, the valve needs attention.

Townhomes, houses and who fixes what

Sky Ranch includes both townhomes and single-family homes. In a townhome community, a homeowners association usually owns shared lines and sets the rules for work that touches them. Ask your association which pipes are yours before you book anything.

For single-family lots, the line from the meter to the house belongs to the owner. Padre Dam Municipal Water District handles the meter and the main. Both districts and associations answer the question of who pays, so ask before the work starts.
